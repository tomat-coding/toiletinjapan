When You Can't Hold It Anymore...

How to Ask to Use a Restaurant or Café Toilet in Japan

Updated September 26, 2026

We've all been there. You confidently skipped the toilet at the train station because "I'll find another one later." Five minutes later, the coffee kicks in and your stomach starts negotiating. If Google Maps shows absolutely nothing, you've entered Emergency Toilet Mode™.

The good news? In Japan, many cafés, restaurants, and convenience stores have customer toilets. The trick is simply asking politely.

☕ First Things First — Is It Okay to Ask?

Usually, yes. If the restaurant has a customer restroom, staff will often let you use it. Some places only allow paying customers, while others are perfectly happy to help if you're polite. If you were planning to buy that coffee anyway, now is an excellent time.

🗺️ Before You Ask: Places Where You Don't Need Permission

Before you try a restaurant, check whether one of these is closer. You can walk straight in:

  • Convenience stores (7-Eleven, FamilyMart, Lawson): most have a customer toilet at the back.
  • Department stores and shopping malls: toilets on every floor, no questions asked.
  • Train stations: check whether the toilet is inside or outside the ticket gates.
  • Large hotels: lobby-level toilets are usually easy to find near the reception or restaurants. Walk in calmly and politely.
  • Chain cafés like Starbucks, Doutor or Tully's: customer toilets, and buying a drink takes a minute.

🙇 Step One: Get Their Attention

Always begin with the magic word:

すみません (Sumimasen)

👉 “Excuse me.” Use this whether you need directions, help, or to interrupt someone because your digestive system has declared war.

🚻 The Best Phrases to Use

The Most Polite Option

お手洗いをお借りしてもいいですか？ (Otearai o okari shite mo ii desu ka?) 👉 “May I use your restroom?”

Literally means "May I borrow your washroom?". No, nobody expects you to return it—this is just how Japanese people naturally ask to use a facility.

Slightly Simpler

トイレをお借りできますか？ (Toire o okari dekimasu ka?) 👉 “May I use your toilet?”

A little shorter, equally polite, and very easy to remember.

😅 If You're Really in Trouble...

Sometimes you don't have time for elegant Japanese grammar. Try this:

お手洗いに行きたいんです。 (Otearai ni ikitai n desu.) 👉 “I need to go to the restroom.”

It's polite but gently communicates that this isn't just "sightseeing" anymore.

🙋 If You're Not a Customer

Being upfront that you haven't ordered yet sounds considerate, and staff are more likely to say yes.

まだ注文していないのですが、お手洗いをお借りしてもいいですか？ (Mada chūmon shite inai no desu ga, otearai o okari shite mo ii desu ka?) 👉 “I haven't ordered yet, but may I use your restroom?”

Staff will usually appreciate the honesty.

🏃 The Universal Emergency Version

If all Japanese disappears from your brain: Smile, bow slightly, and say:

"Toire?" while looking increasingly uncomfortable. Honestly... it works surprisingly often.

👂 How to Recognise a “No”

Japanese staff rarely say a flat “no”. These are the phrases you're likely to hear instead:

すみません、お客様専用なんです。 (Sumimasen, okyakusama sen'yō na n desu.) 👉 “Sorry, it's for customers only.”

Ordering something small usually solves this one.

ちょっと… (Chotto…) 👉 “That's a bit…”

A trailing “chotto” with an apologetic look is a polite no. Thank them and move on.

Staff will often point you somewhere else instead. Listen for コンビニ (konbini, convenience store) or 駅 (eki, station).

🚪 When the Toilet Is Outside the Restaurant

Small bars and izakaya in multi-storey buildings sometimes don't have their own toilet. Instead, there's a shared one in the building's corridor or on another floor. Staff may say トイレは外です (toire wa soto desu, “the toilet is outside”) and point the way, or hand you a key. Just remember to bring the key back.

💡 Important Etiquette Tips

  • Should you buy something? In a café, bakery, or small restaurant, it's good manners to order something (even a ¥150 tea) to show appreciation. Think of it as "rent" for the restroom.
  • What if they say no? Don't take it personally. It might be for staff only, out of order, or the building simply doesn't have one. Just say "Arigatou gozaimasu" and continue your quest.
  • Convenience Stores: Many (especially outside crowded city centers) have customer toilets. Just use the "Toire o okari dekimasu ka?" phrase.
